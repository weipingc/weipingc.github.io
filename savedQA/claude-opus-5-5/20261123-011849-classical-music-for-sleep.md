# Session 2026-11-23 01:18 — Classical music for sleep

## Ask

> what's the best classic music works that help people fall into sleep?

## Answer

Pieces that tend to help with sleep share a few traits: a slow tempo (about 60–80 BPM, close to a resting heart rate), a steady pulse, a narrow dynamic range with no sudden loud climaxes, simple harmony, and soft timbres such as solo piano, strings or harp. Research reviews (for example the 2022 Cochrane review on music for insomnia) find that listening to music like this at bedtime modestly improves how well people say they slept.

### Solo piano (the most reliable choice)

| Work | Composer | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Gymnopédie No. 1 (and Nos. 2–3) | Satie | Very slow, repetitive, almost no dynamic change |
| Clair de lune (Suite bergamasque) | Debussy | Gentle and floating; the middle swell is mild |
| Rêverie | Debussy | Even calmer than Clair de lune |
| Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2 in E-flat; Op. 27 No. 2 | Chopin | Lyrical, rocking left hand |
| Berceuse Op. 57 | Chopin | A literal lullaby built on a repeating rocking bass |
| Träumerei (Kinderszenen No. 7) | Schumann | Short, tender, very soft |
| "Moonlight" Sonata, 1st movement only | Beethoven | Slow, hypnotic triplets (skip the stormy 3rd movement) |
| Goldberg Variations, Aria (and slow variations) | J. S. Bach | Steady and orderly; the insomnia legend behind it is likely a myth, but the Aria works well |
| Spiegel im Spiegel | Arvo Pärt | Modern, minimal, extremely still |

### Better left out of a sleep playlist

Pieces with big climaxes can wake you up even though they are often called "relaxing": Barber's Adagio for Strings, Elgar's Nimrod, Liszt's Liebestraum No. 3, Ravel's Boléro and the final movements of most concertos.

### Practical tips

- Keep the playlist at one even volume and add a sleep timer of about 30–45 minutes.
- Prefer slow, soft recordings. Performances differ a lot; Glenn Gould's 1955 Goldberg Variations, for instance, is far too fast for sleep.
- For this app: the compositions are all public domain, but each recording must be public domain or freely licensed too (Musopen, Wikimedia Commons and similar sources).
